- AI is a science of making machines perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.
- In 1950, Alan Turing asked, “Can machines think?” (Imitation Game ⇒ Machine and Human in two different rooms; question asked: if the judge can't distinguish between the two, the machine wins)
  - The Turing Test
- In 1955, researchers came together with an ambitious belief:
  - In principle, every aspect of learning and intelligence could be described precisely enough for a machine to simulate it.
  - John McCarthy coined the term Artificial Intelligence.
- In 1986, Synthetic Intelligence ⇒ Machines are trying to become intelligent by themselves.
- In 1997, Deep Blue (by IBM) played chess and defeated Garry (the world’s best chess player).
  - Have machines become smarter than Humans?
- Rule-based AI
  - In the 1950s-1980s, intelligence was simply a collection of rules.

- Deep Learning
  - Neural Network ⇒ A **neural network** (or **artificial neural network**) is **a computational model inspired by the human brain that uses interconnected layers of artificial "neurons" to process information**
  - Image Recognition, Speech Recognition, Translation, GPU Revolution + Internet + Datasets
- **Machine Learning (ML):** A broad category of algorithms (such as linear regression, decision trees, and random forests) that learn patterns from historical or structured data to make decisions or predictions.
  - Used for spam email filtering, house price prediction, and personalized recommendation systems on platforms like Spotify or Netflix.
- **Deep Learning (DL):** An advanced subset of ML that uses multi-layered artificial neural networks (resembling the human brain) to solve complex perception problems.
  - Used for facial recognition on smartphones, real-time language translation, autonomous self-driving cars, and generative AI models like ChatGPT or DALL-E
- Computer Vision Revolution ⇒ The dataset, ImageNet ⇒ Large Datasets were trained with this. “Machines could actually see.”
- Natural Language Processing ⇒ The part of AI that lets computers read, understand, and respond to human language, in both text and speech.
  **Example: how your email spam filter works**
  📧 Input: "WIN FREE MONEY NOW! Click here to claim your prize."
  Behind the scenes:
  1️⃣ **Tokenization:** the email is broken into small pieces ("WIN", "FREE", "MONEY")
  2️⃣ **Analysis:** the model looks at the meaning and patterns of those words, not just random letters
  3️⃣ **Classification:** based on past data, it recognizes this as spam
  📁 Output: the email goes to your Spam folder
  **NLP is already in your daily life:**
  🔹 Voice assistants like Siri and Alexa
  🔹 Predictive text on your phone
  🔹 Google Translate
  🔹 Sentiment analysis, where companies scan reviews to see if customers are happy or angry
  - I saw a man with a telescope → Has Multiple Meanings
  - Bag of words ⇒ Categorize as good or bad by words.
  - n-grams ⇒ Taking a single word is not enough.
  - RNN ( Recurrent Neural Network ) ⇒ Major Point. Long sentences are used to find meaning.
  - LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory )
- Transformers (2017) ⇒ “Attention is all you need.” - by Google.
- Large Language Models ⇒ Transformers trained on extremely large data. Need GPU and Data.
  ```markdown
  - An LLM is basically a super-smart autocomplete. You give it some text. It predicts the most likely next word, adds it, and repeats until it has a full answer.
  - Example: "The capital of France is..." → Paris
  - It doesn't "know" this the way we do. It has seen so many patterns in its training data that "Paris" is the most likely next word.
  - Breaking down the name:
    🔹 Large: trained on billions of words from books, websites, and articles
    🔹 Language: it works with human language
    🔹 Model: a pattern-finder, not a human brain

  - What it can do:
    ✅ Chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)
    ✅ Summarize long documents
    ✅ Write text and code
    ✅ Translate between languages
  ```
