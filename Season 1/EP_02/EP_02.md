## Evolution of AI

### What is Artificial Intelligence?

- **Market Impact:** 9 of the top 10 companies by market cap in the world are investing in AI.
- **Capabilities:** Suppose a machine can:
  - Play chess
  - Detect spam
  - Recommend movies
  - Drive a car
  - Write poems
  - Generate a selfie with an actor
- **Definition:** AI is the science of making machines perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

### Historical Milestones

- **1950 – The Turing Test:** Alan Turing asked, _"Can machines think?"_
  - **Imitation Game:** A machine and a human are placed in two different rooms. If the judge cannot distinguish between the two based on responses, the machine wins.
- **1955 – Coining the Term:** Researchers came together with an ambitious belief that, in principle, every aspect of learning and intelligence could be described precisely enough for a machine to simulate it. **John McCarthy** coined the term _Artificial Intelligence_.
- **1986 – Synthetic Intelligence:** Focus shifted toward machines trying to become intelligent by themselves.
- **1997 – Deep Blue:** IBM's computer played chess and defeated Garry Kasparov (the world's best chess player), raising the question: _"Have machines become smarter than humans?"_

## Paradigms of AI

### 1. Rule-Based AI (1950s–1980s)

- Intelligence was simply a collection of hardcoded rules.

### 2. Machine Learning (ML)

- **Shift:** Rules ❌ | Examples ✅
- **Concept:** Training using labeled data (e.g., 1 million labeled pictures of cats vs. dogs) to identify patterns, learn, and make predictions.
- **Limitation:** Requires human aid to prepare and feed the data.
- **Definition:** A broad category of algorithms (such as linear regression, decision trees, and random forests) that learn patterns from historical or structured data to make decisions or predictions.
- **Use Cases:** Spam email filtering, house price prediction, and personalized recommendation systems on platforms like Spotify or Netflix.

### 3. Deep Learning (DL)

- **Neural Network:** A computational model inspired by the human brain that uses interconnected layers of artificial "neurons" to process information.
- **Drivers:** Image recognition, speech recognition, translation, the GPU revolution, the internet, and massive datasets.
- **Definition:** An advanced subset of ML that uses multi-layered artificial neural networks (resembling the human brain) to solve complex perception problems.
- **Use Cases:** Facial recognition on smartphones, real-time language translation, autonomous self-driving cars, and generative AI models like ChatGPT or DALL-E.
- **Computer Vision Revolution:** Driven by the **ImageNet** dataset. Large datasets were trained with this, proving that _"machines could actually see."_

## Natural Language Processing (NLP)

- **Definition:** The part of AI that lets computers read, understand, and respond to human language, in both text and speech.

### Example: How Your Email Spam Filter Works

- **Input:** `"WIN FREE MONEY NOW! Click here to claim your prize."`
- **Behind the Scenes:**
  1. **Tokenization:** The email is broken into small pieces (`"WIN"`, `"FREE"`, `"MONEY"`).
  2. **Analysis:** The model looks at the meaning and patterns of those words, not just random letters.
  3. **Classification:** Based on past data, it recognizes this as spam.
- **Output:** The email goes to your Spam folder.

### NLP in Daily Life

- Voice assistants like Siri and Alexa
- Predictive text on your phone
- Google Translate
- Sentiment analysis (where companies scan reviews to see if customers are happy or angry)

### Evolution of NLP Models

- **Ambiguity Challenge:** Sentences like _"I saw a man with a telescope"_ have multiple meanings.
- **Bag of Words:** Categorizes text as good or bad simply by counting words.
- **n-grams:** Groups words together because taking a single word is not enough for context.
- **RNN (Recurrent Neural Network):** Processes sequences to find meaning in longer sentences.
- **LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory):** Enhances RNNs to retain context over longer text spans.
- **Transformers (2017):** Introduced by Google in the paper _"Attention Is All You Need."_

## Large Language Models & Generative AI

### Large Language Models (LLMs)

- **Overview:** Transformers trained on extremely large datasets requiring massive GPU compute and data.
- **How It Works:** An LLM is basically a super-smart autocomplete. You give it some text, it predicts the most likely next word, adds it, and repeats until it has a full answer.
  - _Example:_ `"The capital of France is..."` → `Paris`
  - It doesn't "know" this the way humans do; it has seen so many patterns in its training data that `"Paris"` is statistically the most likely next word.
- **Breaking Down the Name:**
  - **Large:** Trained on billions of words from books, websites, and articles.
  - **Language:** Works with human language.
  - **Model:** A pattern-finder, not a human brain.
- **Capabilities:**
  - Chat (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)
  - Summarize long documents
  - Write text and code
  - Translate between languages

### Generative AI

- **Definition:** A subfield of artificial intelligence that creates original content—such as text, images, video, audio, and code—by learning patterns from massive training datasets.
- **Paradigm Shift:**
  - **Before:** Classification, prediction, recommendation.
  - **Now:** Generation—creating original poems and articles that do not exist on the internet.

### Modern Milestones

- **The ChatGPT Moment (2022):**
  - Focused on conversational interfaces.
  - OpenAI made it public.
  - Utilized **RLHF** (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback).
  - Improved memory and context handling, leading the modern AI boom.
- **Multimodal AI:** Processing and generating across images, voices, videos, languages, documents, and almost every form of data.
